The reason (re:) exists

Families rarely drift apart all at once.

It happens quietly. People move. Schedules fill up. The group chat gets noisy. The stories stay in one person’s phone. Reunions get harder to organize. The same few relatives keep carrying the work. Younger generations know less of the people and history that came before them.

The problem is not that families stop caring. Staying connected simply gets harder.

01
Connection becomes scattered.

Messages, photos, calendars, family trees, care updates, and plans live in different places — or only in someone’s memory.

02
The family depends on a few people.

The organizers, historians, caregivers, and connectors carry invisible work that is hard to share and easy to lose.

03
Generations lose context.

Names survive. Photos survive. But the relationships, decisions, stories, traditions, and lessons that make a family a family often do not.

Privacy by design

Private family trust is not inventory.

(re:) is designed as private family infrastructure, not a public social network. Sensitive family context is treated as something to protect, not something to monetize through behavioral targeting.

◉
Family-owned context

Family records are designed around tenant isolation and explicit family membership, not open public profiles.

◎
Explicit relationships

Connected families, sponsors, providers, and partners do not gain automatic access to private family information or authority.

◇
Intentional sharing

Private messages, care information, youth activity, financial inputs, and archive content are protected surfaces—not advertising inventory.
